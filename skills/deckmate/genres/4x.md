## References
- Civilization VI — study hex-based districts, adjacency bonuses, and how city planning becomes a territorial decision ([Firaxis analysis](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-civilization-vi-i-s-distinctive-districts-system)).
- Stellaris — study the randomized research deck, early-, mid-, and late-game pacing, and limits on expansion ([Paradox postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-paradox-development-studio-s-i-stellaris-i-)).
- Endless Space 2 — study asymmetric factions, population politics, and testing interconnected systems through Early Access ([Amplitude interview](https://80.lv/articles/endless-space-2-building-4x-space-strategy)).
- Old World — study Orders as a shared administrative resource for movement, diplomacy, and appointments ([Mohawk interview](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1661/game-interview-old-world-by-mohawk-games/)).
## Player communities
- [CivFanatics](https://forums.civfanatics.com/) — look for long campaigns and debates about micromanagement, city strategies, and diplomatic AI behavior.
- [r/Stellaris](https://www.reddit.com/r/Stellaris/) — look for build analysis, expansion pacing, crises, and complaints about the mid and late game.
## Genre pitfalls
- Unchecked expansion creates a runaway leader: the Stellaris postmortem identifies snowballing as an unresolved 4X tension ([source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-paradox-development-studio-s-i-stellaris-i-)).
- A strong opening and spectacular ending do not fill the middle: Paradox explicitly acknowledged shortcomings in the mid-game ([source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-paradox-development-studio-s-i-stellaris-i-)).
- Unlimited movement across many armies inflates late-game micromanagement; Old World constrains it with the shared Orders resource ([source](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1661/game-interview-old-world-by-mohawk-games/)).
