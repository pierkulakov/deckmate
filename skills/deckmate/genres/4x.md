## First playable
- Hypothesis: expansion decisions stay meaningful when territory and resources compete.
- Play a tiny paper map for a few turns; record expansion choices, turn time, and resource gaps.
- Create Heroes for the first settlement and expansion loop; integrate them before adding factions.
- Closest starter: `deckmate project init strategy --data <project>`; adapt its RTS profile to 4X.
## References
- Civilization VI — study hex-based districts, adjacency bonuses, and how city planning becomes a territorial decision ([analysis](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-civilization-vi-i-s-distinctive-districts-system)).
- Stellaris — study the randomized research deck, early-, mid-, and late-game pacing, and limits on expansion ([postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-paradox-development-studio-s-i-stellaris-i-)).
- Endless Space 2 — study asymmetric factions, population politics, and testing interconnected systems through Early Access ([interview](https://80.lv/articles/endless-space-2-building-4x-space-strategy)).
- Old World — study Orders as a shared administrative resource for movement, diplomacy, and appointments ([interview](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1661/game-interview-old-world-by-mohawk-games/)).
## Player communities
- [CivFanatics](https://forums.civfanatics.com/) — look for long campaigns and debates about micromanagement, city strategies, and diplomatic AI behavior.
- [r/Stellaris](https://www.reddit.com/r/Stellaris/) — look for build analysis, expansion pacing, crises, and complaints about the mid and late game.
## Genre pitfalls
- Unchecked expansion creates a runaway leader — snowballing is an unresolved tension of the genre, so keep watching it (example: Stellaris; [source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-paradox-development-studio-s-i-stellaris-i-)).
- A strong opening and spectacular ending do not fill the middle — design and test the mid-game explicitly (example: Stellaris; [source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-paradox-development-studio-s-i-stellaris-i-)).
- Unlimited movement across many armies inflates late-game micromanagement — constrain it with a shared resource covering movement, diplomacy, and appointments (example: Old World's Orders; [source](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1661/game-interview-old-world-by-mohawk-games/)).
