## First playable
- Hypothesis: movement feels precise enough to make a small traversal challenge enjoyable.
- Play a graybox room with one jump; record failed attempts, completion time, and control complaints.
- Create Heroes for movement and the first room; integrate them before adding abilities.
- Start with `deckmate project init platformer --data <project>`.
## References
- Celeste — study iteration across rooms, areas, and the whole game, implicit teaching, and variety that preserves orientation ([GDC](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-the-level-design-of-i-celeste-i-)).
- Super Mario Odyssey — study the dense, vertical New Donk City, varied challenges, and how geometry supports Mario's movement ([analysis](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-level-design-of-super-mario-odyssey-s-best-stage)).
- Super Meat Boy — study the small run/jump move set, variable jump height, and precise movement behavior ([analysis](https://critical-gaming.squarespace.com/blog/2011/1/5/super-meat-boy-pt1.html)).
- Hollow Knight — study connected spaces, landmarks, and organic changes to the plan instead of a rigid lock-and-key sequence ([interview](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/)).

## Genre pitfalls
- Adding variety without reviewing the area and the whole game confuses players — iterate across rooms, areas, and the full game (example: Celeste; [source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-the-level-design-of-i-celeste-i-)).
- Ambiguous feedback makes players blame the controls for their mistakes — treat precision and clarity as explicit goals (example: Hollow Knight; [interview](https://sourcegaming.info/2025/04/09/straight-from-the-source-team-cherry/)).
- One fixed difficulty excludes some players — let players change speed, stamina, dashes, and other rules (example: Celeste's Assist Mode; [analysis](https://gameaccess.info/celeste-motor-accessibility-options/)).
