## References
- Celeste — study iteration across rooms, areas, and the whole game, implicit teaching, and variety that preserves orientation ([GDC](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-the-level-design-of-i-celeste-i-)).
- Super Mario Odyssey — study the dense, vertical New Donk City, varied challenges, and how geometry supports Mario's movement ([analysis](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-level-design-of-super-mario-odyssey-s-best-stage)).
- Super Meat Boy — study the small run/jump move set, variable jump height, and precise movement behavior ([analysis](https://critical-gaming.squarespace.com/blog/2011/1/5/super-meat-boy-pt1.html)).
- Hollow Knight — study connected spaces, landmarks, and organic changes to the plan instead of a rigid lock-and-key sequence ([Team Cherry interview](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/)).

## Genre pitfalls
- Adding variety without reviewing the area and whole game confuses players: Celeste's design iterated across these scales ([source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-the-level-design-of-i-celeste-i-)).
- Ambiguous feedback makes players blame the controls for their mistakes: Team Cherry named precision and clarity as goals for Hollow Knight ([interview](https://sourcegaming.info/2025/04/09/straight-from-the-source-team-cherry/)).
- One fixed difficulty excludes some players: Celeste's Assist Mode lets players change speed, stamina, dashes, and other rules ([analysis](https://gameaccess.info/celeste-motor-accessibility-options/)).
