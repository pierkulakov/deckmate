## References
- Doom Eternal — study how player speed, enemy responsiveness, and arena size are tuned together ([id Software interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-evolving-the-combat-design-of-id-software-s-i-doom-eternal-i-)).
- Titanfall 2 — study chained acceleration, momentum retention, camera tilt, and teaching trust in wall-running ([Respawn interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designer-interview-getting-i-titanfall-i-s-controls-just-right)).
- Half-Life 2 — study redundant navigation cues through light, sound, and NPC movement instead of a single marker ([analysis](https://intermittentmechanism.blog/2018/03/26/lets-study-half-life-2/)).
- VALORANT — study silhouettes against different backgrounds and clear smoke boundaries from different angles ([Riot](https://technology.riotgames.com/news/valorant-shaders-and-gameplay-clarity)).

## Genre pitfalls
- Increasing mobility without redesigning enemies and arenas makes players invulnerable: Doom Eternal strengthened enemy responses alongside dash and meathook ([source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-evolving-the-combat-design-of-id-software-s-i-doom-eternal-i-)).
- Complex movement without camera and geometry cues does not become a habit: Titanfall used level design to teach trust in wall-running ([source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designer-interview-getting-i-titanfall-i-s-controls-just-right)).
- Unclear smoke boundaries and lost silhouettes turn shooting into guesswork; VALORANT preserves gameplay cues at lower quality settings ([source](https://technology.riotgames.com/news/valorant-shaders-and-gameplay-clarity)).
