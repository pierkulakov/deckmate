## References
- Rocket League — study the design decisions behind car and ball handling and the networked physics that make them hold up online ([GDC](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024972/)).
- Super Mega Baseball 2 — study Ego, a 100-step difficulty scale per mechanic instead of easy/medium/hard, and its use for fair online matches across skill levels ([Metalhead interview](https://gamingbolt.com/super-mega-baseball-2-baseball-is-back-with-a-bang)).
- FIFA 22 — study a goalkeeper system rewritten around hundreds of animations and tactical AI for all 22 players ([EA Pitch Notes](https://www.ea.com/ea-play/news/pitch-notes-fifa-22-gameplay-deep-dive)).
- OlliOlli World — study optional landing timing, split routes for newcomers and veterans, and a tone built around flow ([Roll7 interview](https://www.nme.com/features/gaming-features/roll7-co-founders-talk-olliolli-world-its-a-love-letter-to-skateboarding-3148282)).

## Genre pitfalls
- Venue variety that changes the rules splits the competitive scene: Psyonix removed non-standard arenas from ranked play after years of debate ([source](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/rocket-league-is-dropping-non-standard-arenas-for-competitive-season-6)).
- Three difficulty presets leave friends of different skill with no fair game; Metalhead's sliding scale needed real match data to balance ([source](https://gamingbolt.com/super-mega-baseball-2-baseball-is-back-with-a-bang)).
- Physics tuned offline breaks online: Rocket League's handling and its netcode were designed together ([source](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024972/)).
- Timing mechanics that gate completion exclude most players; Roll7 made landing timing optional and split routes by skill ([source](https://www.nme.com/features/gaming-features/roll7-co-founders-talk-olliolli-world-its-a-love-letter-to-skateboarding-3148282)).
