## First playable
- Hypothesis: competing options under pressure create a decision players want to reconsider.
- Play a small paper or graybox scenario; record choices, outcome differences, and decision time.
- Create Heroes for one unit and one map; integrate them into a playable encounter.
- Start with `deckmate project init strategy --data <project>`; adapt its RTS profile when needed.
Sub-genres have their own guides: `rts.md` for real-time strategy, `4x.md` for empire building, and `tactics.md` for turn-based squad combat. Read this file together with the closest of them.
## References
- Civilization IV — study an AI that plays to lose well: a challenge without cheating that breaks role-play and diplomacy ([GDC 2008](https://www.designer-notes.com/playing-to-lose-ai-and-civilization-gdc-2008/)).
- Civilization — study games as a series of interesting decisions and why a secondary technology tree was cut ([GDC 2012](https://gdcvault.com/play/1015756/Interesting)).
- Age of Empires — study design by playing: a rough prototype reshaped through frequent tests with a varied tester base ([GDC talk](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/ensemble-s-shelley-explains-design-by-playing-)).
- Wargroove — study a map editor shared with players, a distinct critical-hit condition for every unit, and naval and air units priced against ground ([interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/an-inside-look-at-i-wargroove-s-i-wicked-design-choices)).
- Frostpunk — study scenarios written from a one-sentence concept, hope and discontent as a balance, and laws that open new dilemmas ([talk](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/6528/igc-2018-empathize-with-numbers-the-process-of-frostpunk-scenario-writing)).

## Genre pitfalls
- An AI that cheats to be hard breaks the role-play — separate AI that plays to win from fun AI that plays to lose (example: Civilization IV; [source](https://www.designer-notes.com/playing-to-lose-ai-and-civilization-gdc-2008/)).
- A decision that is interesting once is not interesting ten times — cut secondary systems that stop producing decisions (example: Civilization's secondary technology tree; [source](https://gdcvault.com/play/1015756/Interesting)).
- Testers who share the team's taste hide problems — recruit a varied test base and act decisively on its feedback (example: Age of Empires; [source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/ensemble-s-shelley-explains-design-by-playing-)).
- Choices without pressure feel optional — balance hope and discontent so laws become necessary (example: Frostpunk's The Arks scenario; [source](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/6528/igc-2018-empathize-with-numbers-the-process-of-frostpunk-scenario-writing)).
