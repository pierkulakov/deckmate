## References
- Don't Starve — study a game-jam prototype grown with its community and the choice to serve a niche, polarizing audience ([GDC](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-how-klei-found-success-with-an-anti-social-game-i-don-t-starve-i-)).
- The Long Dark — study a Condition system that keeps the player searching for resources, learning by trial and error, and the wilderness as antagonist ([Hinterland interview](https://www.vice.com/en/article/the-long-dark-is-a-video-game-where-the-main-character-is-the-wilderness)).
- Subnautica — study open development that turned weak Early Access sales around, nonlinear storytelling, and processing large volumes of player feedback ([GDC postmortem](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025691/-Subnautica)).
- Valheim — study world physicality inspired by Breath of the Wild, building as the hook, and entering Early Access feature-complete and stable ([Iron Gate interview](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/valheim-devs-interview-breath-of-the-wild-skyrim)).

## Genre pitfalls
- Guiding every step removes the discovery survival runs on; The Long Dark leaves learning to trial and error and rewards patience ([source](https://www.vice.com/en/article/the-long-dark-is-a-video-game-where-the-main-character-is-the-wilderness)).
- Entering Early Access unstable or incomplete burns the first impression: Iron Gate waited until Valheim was feature-complete and stable ([source](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/valheim-devs-interview-breath-of-the-wild-skyrim)).
- Softening a niche game for everyone loses the players who love it; Klei found and nurtured the audience for a polarizing design ([source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-how-klei-found-success-with-an-anti-social-game-i-don-t-starve-i-)).
- Weak early sales are not the end: Subnautica recovered through open development and heavy use of player feedback ([source](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025691/-Subnautica)).
