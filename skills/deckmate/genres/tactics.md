## First playable
- Hypothesis: visible threats and limited actions make positioning a meaningful choice.
- Play a paper grid encounter with two units; record decision time, losses, and misunderstood threats.
- Create Heroes for one squad unit and encounter map; integrate movement, attack, and enemy intent.
- Closest starter: `deckmate project init strategy --data <project>`; adapt its RTS profile to tactics.
## References
- XCOM 2 — study displayed hit chances, perceptions of randomness, and the emotional weight of a high-probability miss ([interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/jake-solomon-explains-the-careful-use-of-randomness-in-i-xcom-2-i-)).
- Into the Breach — study telegraphed attacks, deterministic combat, and how information becomes a planning resource ([interview](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?feed=pcgamer&appids=590380&appgroupname=Into+the+Breach&enddate=1520544663)).
- Mario + Rabbids: Kingdom Battle — study dash, team jump, and freely ordering Movement, Attack, and Technique as sources of tactics ([interview](https://www.player.one/mario-rabbids-kingdom-battle-tactics-interview-davide-soliani-119689)).
- Jagged Alliance 3 — study flexible action point spending, targeted body-part shots, and weapon variety ([analysis](https://greatondeck.net/games/jagged-alliance-3-review/)).

## Genre pitfalls
- Hidden adjustments to displayed odds undermine trust in the interface — expect strong reactions to a high-probability miss and keep the displayed odds honest (example: XCOM 2; [source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/jake-solomon-explains-the-careful-use-of-randomness-in-i-xcom-2-i-)).
- Enemy attacks without visible intent turn responses into guesswork — telegraph the threat first, then let the player resolve it (example: Into the Breach; [source](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?feed=pcgamer&appids=590380&appgroupname=Into+the+Breach&enddate=1520544663)).
- Movement treated only as the cost of reaching a position loses combinations — make movement a tactical action in its own right (example: Mario + Rabbids: Kingdom Battle; [source](https://www.player.one/mario-rabbids-kingdom-battle-tactics-interview-davide-soliani-119689)).
